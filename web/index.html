    It works like a real exam board

    Sealed papers, a locked exam room, impartial marking and published results. The difference is that hardware and a public ledger enforce the rules, not a promise.

    1. An expert writes a sealed exam

      A nurse, a tax adviser or an engineer writes questions with one right answer. They're locked away so nobody can read them, not even us, and the writer is paid every time they're used.

    2. The AI sits it in a locked room

      The exam is only ever opened inside secure hardware, and put to the AI there. Claude, GPT, Gemini or Qwen: the same rules for every maker.

    3. A machine marks it, and the result is public

      Plain code checks every answer against the key. The score goes on NEAR, a public record nobody can edit, so anyone can check it.

    Questions people ask

    Who is this for?

    Anyone who has to choose an AI and can't afford to be wrong: a hospital, a law firm, a bank, a school. It's also for experts who want to be paid for writing good questions in their field.

    Is the money in the wallet test real?

    It's real NEAR on NEAR's test network, so it has no cash value, but every transfer is a real transaction anyone can look up. When an AI approves a scam, the contract moves 0.05 NEAR from that AI's wallet to the scammer's account in the same step that records the mark.

    Can I test Claude, GPT or Gemini?

    Yes. Proctor reaches them through NEAR AI Cloud, and the app lets anyone run them today. Those models run on their makers' own servers, so the maker sees the questions. Proctor records that, and marks the set as seen by that company for good. Models hosted inside NEAR AI's secure hardware are the only ones nobody sees.

    Can Proctor read the questions or change a score?

    No. We never hold the questions in readable form, and scores are stored by a public contract that checks each one came from the right marking program and the right sealed set.

    What if a question or its answer is wrong?

    Once a set is published, anyone can challenge an answer with a small deposit. If the challenge holds, they're paid from the writer's stake, and every score is recounted without that question.

    Do I need cryptocurrency?

    Not real money. Proctor runs on NEAR's test network, where tokens are free. A free MyNearWallet account is enough to run an exam.

    Is it live?

    Yes, with real AI models. Every number on this page is read from the contract when you open it. It hasn't had a security audit yet.
