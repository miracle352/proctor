    How a sealed exam works

    The same rules that make a school exam fair, enforced by hardware and a public ledger instead of a teacher's promise.

    1. An expert writes the questions

      A tax adviser, a nurse, an engineer. Each question has one exact answer, and the writer earns a fee every time it is used.

    2. The questions are sealed

      They're encrypted so that only one exact build of Proctor's marking program can open them, and only inside secure hardware. Not us, and not the AI's maker.

    3. A fingerprint goes on NEAR first

      Before any AI sees a question, a fingerprint of the whole set is written to NEAR with a stake behind the answer key. The set can't be swapped or quietly edited later.

    4. The AI sits the exam behind a locked door

      Inside the hardware the questions are opened and put to the AI. Claude, GPT, Gemini or an open model: the rules are the same for every maker.

    5. Plain code marks it, and the score is public

      No second AI does the judging. Only the score leaves the hardware, and it's stored on NEAR where anyone can check it. The writer is paid in the same step.

    How Proctor catches an AI that memorised the answers

    Every published test question gets a sealed twin: the same maths with fresh names and fresh numbers of the same size. An AI that really reasons scores the same on both. One that memorised the internet does better on the version it has seen.

    Questions people ask

    Who is this for?

    Anyone who has to choose an AI and can't afford to be wrong: a hospital, a law firm, a bank, a school. It's also for experts who want to be paid for writing good questions in their field.

    Can I test Claude, GPT or Gemini?

    Yes. Proctor reaches them through NEAR AI Cloud, and the app lets anyone run them today. Those models run on their makers' own servers, so the maker sees the questions. Proctor records that, and marks the set as seen by that company for good. Models hosted inside NEAR AI's secure hardware are the only ones nobody sees.

    Can Proctor read the questions or change a score?

    No. We never hold the questions in readable form, and scores are stored by a public contract that checks each one came from the right marking program and the right sealed set.

    What if a question or its answer is wrong?

    Once a set is published, anyone can challenge an answer with a small deposit. If the challenge holds, they're paid from the writer's stake, and every score is recounted without that question.

    Do I need cryptocurrency?

    Not real money. Proctor runs on NEAR's test network, where tokens are free. A free MyNearWallet account is enough to run an exam.

    Is it live?

    Yes, with real AI models. Every number on this page is read from the contract when you open it. It hasn't had a security audit yet.

    Before you trust an AI's score, ask whether it had seen the test.
